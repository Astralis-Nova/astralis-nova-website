The Lazarus investigation

Four days in the tomb

John describes Lazarus being brought out of the tomb after four days. John 12 places Lazarus at a meal afterward and records the reaction his presence provoked. But the Bible does not say how or when Lazarus eventually died.

The resurrection yet to come

These passages describe future hope, visions, and judgment. They are not added to the ten past events above. Interpretations of their order and imagery differ among Jewish and Christian traditions.

The two witnesses

Revelation portrays two witnesses who are killed and stand again after three and a half days. It is part of an apocalyptic vision, and interpreters disagree on whether its imagery should be read literally or symbolically.

A question worth examining

Restored life or resurrection beyond death?

Returned to ordinary life

In the accounts of Lazarus, the widow's sons, Tabitha, and others, the people return to earthly life. The text does not describe these people as having become immortal.

Jesus's resurrection

The New Testament makes a distinct theological claim about Jesus: he is raised never to die again (Romans 6:9), central to Christian faith. This is a statement of the text and its theology, not a claim that historical research can settle the supernatural question.

What the stories do not tell us

Did Lazarus describe the afterlife? John records no such description. How many people arose in Matthew 27? No number is given. How long did each person live afterward? Usually not stated. These unanswered questions deserve to remain unanswered instead of being filled with invention.
