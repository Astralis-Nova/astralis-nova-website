DANIEL 6 • COURAGE UNDER PRESSURE

Daniel and the Lions’ Den

Daniel’s courage was not loud or reckless. He remained faithful when fear demanded that he become someone else.

The Story

King Darius ruled over a vast kingdom. To help govern it, he appointed 120 officials called satraps, and over them he placed three administrators. Daniel was one of those three.

Daniel quickly stood out. He was dependable, wise, and honest. The king trusted him so much that he considered placing Daniel over the entire kingdom.

That made the other officials jealous.

They began searching for something they could use against Daniel. They examined his work, hoping to find corruption, negligence, or dishonesty. But they found nothing. Daniel was faithful in his duties and gave them no legitimate reason to accuse him.

So they decided that the only way to trap Daniel was through his devotion to God.

The officials approached King Darius and praised him. Then they proposed a new law:

For thirty days, no one in the kingdom should pray to any god or person except the king. Anyone who disobeyed would be thrown into a den of lions.

The officials urged Darius to sign the decree under the law of the Medes and Persians, which could not easily be changed once established.

The king agreed and signed it.

When Daniel learned about the decree, he did not hide his faith. He went home, entered the upstairs room where his windows opened toward Jerusalem, and prayed to God three times a day, just as he had always done.

Daniel did not pray to create a public spectacle. He simply refused to abandon his regular devotion because of fear.

The officials had expected this. They went together and found Daniel praying and asking God for help.

They hurried back to the king and reminded him of the decree. Then they accused Daniel, saying that he still prayed three times a day.

King Darius was deeply distressed. He did not want Daniel harmed and spent the rest of the day trying to find a lawful way to rescue him.

But the officials reminded the king that a decree made under their laws could not be revoked.

At last, Darius was forced to order Daniel thrown into the lions’ den.

Before Daniel was lowered inside, the king said, “May your God, whom you continually serve, rescue you.”

A large stone was placed over the opening. The king sealed it with his own signet ring and with the rings of his nobles so that no one could secretly alter Daniel’s fate.

That night, the king returned to his palace. He refused food and entertainment. He could not sleep.

At the first light of dawn, Darius hurried to the lions’ den.

As he approached, he called out anxiously, “Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you continually serve, been able to rescue you from the lions?”

From inside the den, Daniel answered, “May the king live forever. My God sent his angel and shut the mouths of the lions. They have not hurt me, because I was found innocent before God. I have also done nothing wrong against you, Your Majesty.”

The king was overjoyed. He ordered Daniel lifted out of the den. When Daniel was examined, no wound was found on him because he had trusted in God.

Then Darius ordered that the men who had falsely accused Daniel be punished. According to the ancient account, they and their families were thrown into the lions’ den.

Afterward, King Darius issued a proclamation throughout his kingdom. He commanded the people to respect the God of Daniel.

The king declared that Daniel’s God was living and enduring, that his kingdom would not be destroyed, and that he rescued and performed wonders.

Daniel continued to prosper during the reign of Darius and later under Cyrus of Persia.

Meaning of the Story

Daniel’s courage was not loud or reckless. He simply remained faithful when fear demanded that he become someone else.

  • Integrity matters even when nobody rewards it.
  • Jealous people may attack what they cannot corrupt.
  • Faithfulness does not guarantee avoidance of danger.
  • Courage can mean continuing an ordinary good habit under extraordinary pressure.
  • Political power has limits, but conscience should not be casually surrendered.
