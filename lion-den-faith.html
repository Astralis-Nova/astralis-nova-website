DANIEL 6 • COURAGE UNDER PRESSURE

Daniel and the Lions’ Den

A story of integrity, prayer, courage, and a calm heart surrounded by danger.

The Story

King Darius ruled over a vast kingdom. To help govern it, he appointed 120 officials called satraps, and over them he placed three administrators. Daniel was one of those three.

Daniel quickly stood out. He was dependable, wise, and honest. The king trusted him so much that he considered placing Daniel over the entire kingdom.

The other officials became jealous. They searched Daniel’s work for corruption, negligence, or dishonesty, but they found nothing.

So they decided that the only way to trap Daniel was through his devotion to God.

They persuaded King Darius to sign a decree saying that for thirty days, no one could pray to any god or person except the king. Anyone who disobeyed would be thrown into a den of lions.

When Daniel learned about the decree, he went home and prayed three times a day, just as he had always done. He did not pray to create a spectacle. He simply refused to abandon his devotion because of fear.

The officials found Daniel praying and accused him before the king. Darius was deeply distressed and tried to find a lawful way to rescue Daniel, but the decree could not be revoked.

At last, Daniel was thrown into the lions’ den. Before he was lowered inside, the king said, “May your God, whom you continually serve, rescue you.”

A large stone was placed over the opening and sealed. That night the king refused food and could not sleep.

At dawn, Darius hurried to the den and called out, “Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God been able to rescue you from the lions?”

Daniel answered, “My God sent his angel and shut the mouths of the lions. They have not hurt me.”

The king was overjoyed and ordered Daniel lifted from the den. No wound was found on him because he had trusted in God.

Afterward, Darius issued a proclamation honoring Daniel’s God as living and enduring, one who rescues and performs wonders. Daniel continued to prosper during the reign of Darius and later under Cyrus of Persia.

Meaning of the Story

  • Integrity matters even when nobody rewards it.
  • Jealous people may attack what they cannot corrupt.
  • Faithfulness does not guarantee avoidance of danger.
  • Courage can mean continuing an ordinary good habit under extraordinary pressure.
  • Political power has limits, but conscience should not be casually surrendered.

Scripture: Daniel, chapter 6. 🦁📜
